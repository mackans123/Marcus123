## What is website visitor identification software (and how does it work)?

Website visitor identification software tells you which organisations (and, in some tools, which people) visit your website, even when they never fill in a form. It turns anonymous sessions into named accounts you can score, route to sales and engage.

Most B2B buyers research quietly. In our own sales conversations, the most common first question is some version of "which companies are actually on our site, and are they relevant?" One UK manufacturing CMO put it this way when describing what they needed: a tool with "strong filtering tools to help us separate the noise from the genuine leads."

There are two technical approaches, and the difference matters more than any feature list.

### Company-level identification (reverse IP to company)

A small script on your site records each visit's IP address. The tool matches that IP against a database of IP ranges registered to or used by businesses, then names the company and enriches it with firmographics such as size, industry, revenue, location and technology used. Better tools add on-site behaviour (which pages, how often, how long) and off-site intent data to score how close the account is to buying.

Company-level identification works globally because it doesn't need to know who the person is. Its limits are honest ones: home-office and mobile traffic often resolves to an internet service provider (ISP) instead of an employer, and VPNs hide the real network. Buyers told us this noise is a real cost. A growth partner at an analytics consultancy complained that "a significant portion of the identified visitors are showing up as Internet Service Providers… our monthly identification quota is being used on companies that are not useful to us."

### Person-level identification (identity graphs)

Person-level tools try to name the individual. They match the visitor's browser to an identity graph built from cookies, hashed emails and partner data networks, and return a name, LinkedIn profile or business email. Because this relies on consumer data that is legal to process without consent in parts of the US, most person-level tools restrict it to US traffic. RB2B's pricing page, for example, states that "Contact-level (person) ID is US only." <!-- src: https://www.rb2b.com/pricing, checked 2026-11-02 --> Apollo says the same about its contact-level visitor identification. <!-- src: https://www.apollo.io/product/website-visitor-identification, checked 2026-11-02 -->

### Visitor tracking vs visitor identification

Website visitor *tracking* software, such as Google Analytics 4, counts sessions, sources and page views; it deliberately does not tell you who the visitor is. Website visitor *identification* software names the company (or person) behind the session. Most B2B teams run both: analytics for traffic, an identification tool for pipeline.

## Company-level vs person-level: which do you need?

Choose company-level identification if you sell to accounts, have meaningful European or UK traffic, or need an answer your legal team will accept. Choose person-level identification if most of your pipeline is in the US and your SDRs want named people to call today.

| Question | Company-level fits better | Person-level fits better |
|---|---|---|
| Where is your traffic? | EU, UK, Nordics, DACH, global | Mostly US |
| Legal risk appetite | Low: legal reviews tracking scripts | Higher: US-only, consumer-data graphs accepted |
| Sales motion | Account-based: buying committees, ABM, marketing + SDR | 1:1 SDR follow-up on named individuals |
| What you do next | Find the right decision-makers at the account, score by intent, run ABM ads | Email or call the specific visitor |
| Typical tools | Albacross, Leadfeeder, Snitcher, Leadinfo, Lead Forensics, SalesViewer | RB2B, Warmly, Instantly, Happierleads |

If most of your pipeline is US and your SDRs want named people to call today, a person-level tool (RB2B, Warmly) fits better. Albacross identifies companies, then finds the right decision-makers with verified contact credits.

## Why B2B teams buy (or switch) visitor identification tools

These are the reasons buyers gave us in sales calls, trials and support conversations between May and November 2026, in their own words where we have them (anonymised).

1. **"Analytics shows visits, not companies."** Teams want to see which accounts are researching them without waiting for a form fill.
2. **Noise eats the quota.** ISPs, mobile carriers, VPN and hosting traffic, and bots. One UK sales agency asked for a tool that will "filter out ISPs, mobile carriers, VPN/hosting traffic and bots so our reps aren't chasing noise."
3. **Manual work.** Incumbent workflows that involve downloading CSVs and copying data between spreadsheets for leadership dashboards, or, as one Danish office-technology marketer described it, a manual hand-off to sales of about five minutes per lead.
4. **Manual page tagging and per-site pricing.** A logistics property group evaluating a switch said its incumbent required manual tagging of more than 240 property pages.
5. **Unclear limits and pricing.** "Did we hit the limit, or not?" is a real support question, as is "is it billed per identified company per month?"
6. **Cookie consent suppresses identification, and legal wants answers.** Several EU buyers found their incumbent only fired after cookie acceptance, which lowered volume, and their legal teams sent written questions.
7. **"Will it pay off at our scale?"** A Swedish HR SaaS marketing manager asked for "an honest assessment of whether this makes sense at our scale, and whether there's a small pilot."
8. **"Our CRM already does it."** Some HubSpot users decide the native feature is enough. That is a legitimate choice, and we cover it under Breeze Intelligence below.

## Is website visitor identification legal under GDPR?

Company-level website visitor identification can be run lawfully under GDPR in most set-ups, because it identifies organisations rather than people. But the tracking script, any personal data that is processed along the way (an IP address can be personal data) and any follow-up outreach each need a legal basis. Person-level identification of EU visitors is much harder to justify, which is why most person-level tools limit it to US traffic. [[VERIFY with legal: approve this entire section before publishing.]]

What to check, in order:

1. **The legal basis.** Most company-level vendors rely on legitimate interest under [Article 6(1)(f) GDPR](https://gdpr-info.eu/art-6-gdpr/): processing that is "necessary for the purposes of the legitimate interests pursued by the controller or by a third party," unless overridden by the individual's rights. Leadinfo and Snitcher both state this basis publicly. <!-- src: https://www.leadinfo.com/en/pricing/, checked 2026-11-02 --> <!-- src: https://www.snitcher.com/pricing, checked 2026-11-02 --> You still need a documented legitimate interest assessment.
2. **The script and cookie consent.** Storing or reading information on a visitor's device falls under [Article 5(3) of the ePrivacy Directive](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/dir/2002/58/oj), and the European Data Protection Board's [Guidelines 2/2023 on the technical scope of Art. 5(3)](https://www.edpb.europa.eu/our-work-tools/our-documents/guidelines/guidelines-22023-technical-scope-art-53-eprivacy-directive_en) (final version published 2024-10-16) explain how broadly that applies. Decide with your legal team whether your script runs before or after consent. Buyers in our sample found that consent-gating lowered identification volume, so ask vendors which set-ups they support. [[VERIFY with legal: Albacross's approved public wording on cookieless, hybrid and consent-gated set-ups.]]
3. **A Data Processing Agreement.** Your vendor should sign a DPA. Albacross's is at [/data-processing-agreement](/data-processing-agreement); see also [Albacross and GDPR](/newsroom/albacross-gdpr).
4. **Data hosting and sub-processors.** Ask where data is stored and who processes it. Buyers in our sample raised this specifically about US data vendors. [[VERIFY: Albacross hosting location for public use]]
5. **Outreach.** Contacting decision-makers at identified companies is a separate processing activity with its own rules (and national e-marketing laws). Treat it separately from identification.

Why do US person-level tools exclude EU visitors? Because naming an individual from an identity graph built on consumer data needs a lawful basis that is hard to establish for EU residents. RB2B and Apollo both limit contact-level identification to US visitors. <!-- src: https://www.rb2b.com/pricing, checked 2026-11-02 --> <!-- src: https://www.apollo.io/product/website-visitor-identification, checked 2026-11-02 -->

## How to test match rates before you buy

Every vendor publishes a different "identification rate": Leadinfo says around 30% of traffic, RB2B claims 35–45% US coverage on Pro+, Factors says over 75% of companies. Those numbers measure different things on different traffic. The only fair comparison is on your own site. Wins and losses in our own pipeline were decided by exactly this kind of test.

1. **Run two (or three) trials at the same time** for 14 days on the same website. Most tools here offer a 14-day trial.
2. **Install every script the same way** (same tag manager, same consent behaviour), or you'll compare consent set-ups instead of tools.
3. **Count unique companies identified,** not sessions or "visits".
4. **Remove noise:** ISPs, mobile carriers, hosting/VPN networks, bots and your own company.
5. **Measure ICP fit:** of the remaining companies, how many match your target accounts list or ideal customer profile? This matters more than raw volume.
6. **Spot-check false positives:** pick 20 identified companies and check whether the visit pattern makes sense.
7. **Split by region:** compare results for EU, UK and US traffic separately; tools differ most here.
8. **Test the workflow:** time how long it takes to get an identified account into your CRM, a Slack or Teams alert, and an outreach sequence.

## FAQ

### What is the best website visitor identification software?

It depends on your market. For European or GDPR-sensitive B2B teams that want company-level identification, intent scoring and outreach in one tool with a published price, Albacross is the best fit. For person-level identification of US visitors, choose RB2B or Warmly. To start free, use Leadfeeder Lite or RB2B's free plan.

### Can I see exactly who visits my website?

You can usually see which company visited, not which person. Company-level tools match the visitor's IP address to a business. Person-level tools name individuals only for US traffic, using identity graphs, and most exclude EU visitors for legal reasons. For EU traffic, the practical route is to identify the company and then find the right decision-makers.

### Is website visitor identification GDPR compliant?

Company-level identification can be GDPR compliant when it has a documented legal basis (usually legitimate interest under Art. 6(1)(f)), a lawful script and consent set-up, and a signed DPA with the vendor. Person-level identification of EU residents is much harder to justify. Ask each vendor for its legal basis, DPA and hosting location in writing. [[VERIFY with legal]]

### How accurate is website visitor identification?

Accuracy varies by tool, region and traffic mix, and published figures are vendor claims. Examples checked on 2026-11-02: Leadinfo says around 30% of traffic, RB2B claims 35–45% US person-level coverage on Pro+, and Omron identified 28% of visitors with Albacross versus 4% with its previous tool. Remote workers, VPNs and mobile networks lower match rates for every IP-based tool. Run a side-by-side trial on your own traffic to get a real number.

### Can I identify website visitors for free?

Yes, with limits. Leadfeeder Lite shows your last 100 identified companies a month with 7 days of history; RB2B's free plan gives 150 company-level resolutions a month; Apollo includes company identification on its free plan; and Instantly gives 25 free credits. ### Does visitor identification work with remote workers and VPNs?

Partly. Company-level tools rely on business IP addresses, so visitors on home broadband, mobile networks or VPNs often resolve to an ISP rather than their employer. Good tools let you filter that noise out. Intent data and repeat visits from office networks help fill the gaps, but no IP-based tool identifies every remote visitor.

### Can I automatically contact companies that visit my website?

Yes. Albacross Engage can start AI-personalised email and LinkedIn sequences to decision-makers at high-intent visiting companies, using verified contact credits, from the Starter plan. Warmly, Apollo and Instantly offer similar follow-up in the US. In the EU, B2B outreach has its own legal requirements, separate from identification, so check national e-marketing rules.
